## Summary
The construction arm of the Fishbone Group and a wholly owned subsidiary of Fishbone Holdings Ltd. Formerly named Fishbone Drylining Ltd; the name changed by special resolution on 31 October 2024. Its accounts still describe the principal activity as drylining. Turnover fell by about 64% in the year to 29 April 2025 to £849,309 and the company made a small loss after tax, while investing £449,536 in leasehold improvements and paying a £128,000 dividend to Holdings. It carries the majority of the group's external borrowing.

## Key facts
- Company number 07948220, incorporated in England and Wales, private company limited by shares. Registered office 6 Beverley Place, Wallsend NE28 7BH. [S1]
- Directors: M Gaudiesius and A Prutkovas. Two employees including directors. Accountants RMT; bankers HSBC. [S1]
- Name changed from Fishbone Drylining Ltd to Fishbone Construction Ltd by special resolution on 31 October 2024. Same legal entity, as the Loans Wiki had already established. [S1 directors' report][S3]
- Wholly owned subsidiary of Fishbone Holdings Ltd (the accounts say "the member", singular). Exempt from consolidation as part of a small group. [S1 note 11][S2 note 1.1]
- Accounting reference date 29 April (one day earlier than the other group companies' 30 April). [S1]
- One of three group borrowing entities tracked in the Loans Wiki; carried about £517,196 of facilities and £23,706 per month as at 21 Aug 2026. [S3]
- Operational trades historically covered: drylining, joinery, decorating, glazing partitions, floor sanding, painting, tiling, plumbing, electrics, fencing, roofing, gutters, mastic, drainage, LVT and carpet installation. [S5]
- Owner pay target referenced at £2k to £3k per month; £3,750 per month reported at the 18 Aug 2026 check-in. Directors' remuneration in the accounts was £46,772 for FY2025 (2024: £74,400). [S5][S1 p.15]

### Observations from the FY2025 accounts
- Revenue fell from £2.35m to £0.85m while the company capitalised £449,536 of leasehold improvements and £46,772 of software development. Combined with the £128,000 dividend, cash stayed thin (£20,938) and bank borrowing tripled. This is consistent with the Loans Wiki's picture of heavy short-term lending in 2024 to 2026.
- The leasehold improvements and the plant and machinery (which includes the woodworking machines under Haydock hire purchase) may relate to the furniture operation, which trades through a separate subsidiary. Which company owns the workshop lease and the machines is an open question shared with the Amfa Furniture article.
- **The woodworking machinery entered the books in FY2024, not FY2025.** The FY2024 accounts and CT600 show £294,729 of additions qualifying for the annual investment allowance, including the panel-shop machines bought from R&J Machinery on 9 November 2023 (Inventair MK1 and MK2, a Vitap K2, an Altendorf F45 panel saw and a Hebrock edgebander, about £181,595 together), a £29,000 Transit van, pneumatic tools and a fork truck, plus £67,774 of software development (WeAreWay Ltd). So the machinery behind OI-8 was capitalised in Fishbone Construction's books in the year to 29 April 2024. [S11 note 4][S13]

### Live bank-data view (from the Construction Ltd Knowledge Base, SRC-08, cited not copied)
Construction's own dedicated knowledge base is not a document library like the others: it is a **cost-estimating / invoice-processing workspace** whose brain is a Smartsheet rate database, the "Сlassifier" (1,116 rows; items coded L/M/P for Labour/Material/Plant; projects coded `FP <n>_<address>`, invoices `FC<n>`). It has no CLAUDE.md and no standing dashboards; its Outputs folder is empty. Its group-relevant financial content is a set of **raw HSBC business-current-account statements (Feb–Jul 2026)** in `Raw/Finance` plus the change-of-name certificate. Facts below are read from those statements — transaction flow, not reconciled balances; individual salary and subcontractor payments were not extracted (personal). [S20]
- **Construction is the group's intercompany-lending hub.** The statements show near-daily, round-sum transfers labelled "loan" / "loan payback" / "PAYBACK" in both directions with Fishbone Properties Ltd, Fishbone Holdings and Fishbone Commercial, and also with **Anthill Homes Ltd** (out of group scope per the 2026-09-03 owner note, but a live cash counterparty) and **AT UK Interiors Ltd** (a £15,000 credit on 22 Jul 2026; confirmed **not** connected to the group per the 2026-09-05 owner note — an ordinary third-party transaction, not an intercompany balance). The inconsistent labelling in both directions is exactly the raw material behind OI-6, and cannot be netted to balances from the bank alone. [S20]
- **External facilities drawn through this account:** several Funding Circle facilities at once (including a new **£59,451 drawdown on 29 Jul 2026**), an iwoca revolving facility (drawdowns plus a £20,000 repayment on 30 Jul 2026), Haydock Finance HP, MotoNovo vehicle finance, an NCFF6 commercial-finance collection, and an **HMRC Time-to-Pay (NDDS) direct debit** — consistent with the accounts' tripling of bank borrowing and this company carrying most of the group's borrowing. Cash is tight, the balance repeatedly dipping to a few hundred pounds between receipts. Both directors also inject director loans (e.g. a £25,000 A Prutkovas director-loan credit on 29 Jun 2026). [S20]
- **OI-8 — the joinery/furniture trade runs through Fishbone Construction's own account.** The statements show it buying woodworking machinery (R&J Machinery), sheet materials (SP Uniboards, MKM) and furniture hardware/fittings (Häfele, Interfit Furniture, Markfield Woodwork), and **receiving large recurring payments from MacDonald Joinery** (a major customer) plus one-off furniture/desk sales. "AMFA" / Furniture by Fishbone is not named as a counterparty. So, as at 2026, the furniture-manufacturing trade — and its machinery spend — is running through Fishbone Construction Ltd, not through the Amfa/Furniture-by-Fishbone entity. This is the strongest evidence yet on OI-8, though it still does not say which company holds the workshop *lease* or legal *title* to the machines. [S20]
- **SSAS:** two standing orders of £50.08/month (one for each director) run to "Fishbone SSAS" (~£100/month total). The SSAS loanback itself (the £790.13/month repayments) runs through Fishbone Commercial Properties' Starling account, not this one, and is not evidenced here. [S20]

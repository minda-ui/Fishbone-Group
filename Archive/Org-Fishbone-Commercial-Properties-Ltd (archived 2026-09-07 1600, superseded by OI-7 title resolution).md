## Summary
The group's commercial property company, a separate legal entity from Fishbone Properties Ltd and, like it, under common control with Fishbone Holdings Ltd rather than a subsidiary. It owns the freehold of 145 High Street East, Wallsend, whose retail unit is let to Food Land, and is carrying out structural works on the building. Its only external borrowing is a loanback from the group's SSAS pension scheme (its FY2025 accounts show "secured loans" of £41,500, exactly the loanback's original principal), but it owed Construction £97,319 and Properties Ltd £103,251 at the last dates recorded. Its own FY2025 statutory accounts — 18 months to 30 April 2025 — have now been read from the Finance archive: a small trading company living on £18,000 of rent, £9,148 profit for the period and net assets of £10,562, its £240,559 building funded almost entirely by intercompany and pension borrowing. A dedicated knowledge base and Smartsheet workspace were started for it on 3 Sep 2026.

[S13][S6 note 9][S7 note 10]

- Turnover is the Food Land rent (£1,000/month, roughly 18 months). Administrative expenses break down as insurance £1,952, legal and professional £2,352, accountancy £2,800, subscriptions £417 and loan arrangement fees £395. Two employees (the directors). [S13]
- **Balance sheet at 30 Apr 2025.** Investment property £240,559, carried at cost (all additions in the period, no revaluation — the RICS £150,000 figure is not booked). Debtors £50; cash £19,775. Creditors due within one year £215,758 (corporation tax £2,146, other tax and social security £386, and other creditors £213,226 which itself includes the £7,436 current portion of the SSAS secured loan). Net current liabilities £195,933. Creditors due after one year £34,064 (the non-current SSAS secured loan). **Net assets £10,562.** [S13]
- The corporation tax return (CT600) covers the tax accounting period 1 Nov 2023 to 31 Oct 2024; box order is scrambled in the scanned copy. [S14]
- **Going concern.** The accounts are prepared on a going-concern basis. The company is balance-sheet solvent only by a thin margin (£10,562) and has large net current liabilities, but those current creditors are overwhelmingly the interest-free, no-fixed-term intercompany balances owed to Construction and Properties Ltd, i.e. group funding rather than third-party demand debt; the directors sign off on that basis. [S13]

### From the Commercial Properties Ltd Knowledge Base (SRC-10, cited not copied)
The company has its own dedicated knowledge base (root created 3 Sep 2026): a structured Wiki (Finance, Properties, Tenants, Suppliers, People, Processes, Decisions), a standing `kb-registers.md`, a 28-item missing-documents checklist and ~20 dated change-log entries. No automation is live yet (six routines are proposed); access is owner-only. Its current CLAUDE.md is a 29 KB "v2" (2026-09-03, corrected to 2026-09-05) — the successor to the archived v1 cited as [S1], now found on Drive. Facts below are from that KB. [S15]
- **CT600 detail.** The return covers only the first 12 months (1 Nov 2023 to 31 Oct 2024): property income about £9,974, profits chargeable to tax £7,813, corporation tax **£1,484.47 — shown still unpaid at signing** (declaration 30 Apr 2026, roughly eight months after the normal due date). Box 326 declares **four associated companies** — the first hard evidence the group is larger than the three entities this KB names, so at least one associated company is unidentified from its side. [S16]
- **The building runs as a cash conduit.** Its Starling account opened at £19,775 (May 2025) and was down to £41.30 by Sep 2026; the only external income is the Food Land rent (£21,610 over the period). Rent is swept to Construction (labelled "Drylining") as loan payback within days, and about £800 comes back around the 28th so the monthly SSAS payment clears. [S17]
- **The structural works are funded by another group company.** No contractor payments appear in this company's own bank account. The structural engineer is Studio Structure Ltd (invoices SI-2281 £2,100 and SI-2296 £1,020, plus a £662.40 North Tyneside Council fee); the main works spend runs through another group entity, creating a further undocumented intercompany claim — who pays and how it is booked is open. [S18]
- **Valuation caveats.** The RICS £150,000 market value is the **ground-floor retail freehold only**, on a special assumption that the structural repairs are complete and certified (they are not); the report's three-month validity lapsed on 5 Jun 2025, and the freehold reversion of the flats above was not valued. [S19]
- **The QuickBooks connector points at the wrong company.** Checked 2026-09-04, the Intuit connector returns "Fishbone Properties Ltd", so no live QuickBooks figure can be taken for this company — the accounts are the financial source. It nonetheless pays Intuit about £40–46/month, possibly funding the sister company's QuickBooks file (an intercompany cost sitting in the wrong entity). [S20]
- **On the £213,226 "other creditors":** this KB reads it as the funding behind the £240,559 property purchase rather than as the SSAS loan, and — working only from its own bank flows — records the counterparties as "Drylining" (i.e. Construction), Properties Ltd and "HOLDINGS" without itemised balances. That is consistent with the £97,319 and £103,251 owed to Construction and Properties per those companies' own accounts (together ≈ £200,570). [S21]
- Suppliers and their roles named in the KB: RMT (accountants), Cosey Homes Ltd (RICS valuer), Studio Structure Ltd (structural engineer), BQI Group (insurance broker). [S15]

## Summary
The construction arm of the Fishbone Group and a wholly owned subsidiary of Fishbone Holdings Ltd. Formerly named Fishbone Drylining Ltd; the name changed by special resolution on 31 October 2024. Its accounts still describe the principal activity as drylining. Turnover fell by about 64% in the year to 29 April 2025 to £849,309 and the company made a small loss after tax, while investing £449,536 in leasehold improvements and paying a £128,000 dividend to Holdings. It carries the majority of the group's external borrowing.

## Key facts
- Company number 07948220, incorporated in England and Wales, private company limited by shares. Registered office 6 Beverley Place, Wallsend NE28 7BH. [S1]
- Directors: M Gaudiesius and A Prutkovas. Two employees including directors. Accountants RMT; bankers HSBC. [S1]
- Name changed from Fishbone Drylining Ltd to Fishbone Construction Ltd by special resolution on 31 October 2024. Same legal entity, as the Loans Wiki had already established. [S1 directors' report][S3]
- Wholly owned subsidiary of Fishbone Holdings Ltd (the accounts say "the member", singular). Exempt from consolidation as part of a small group. [S1 note 11][S2 note 1.1]
- Accounting reference date 29 April (one day earlier than the other group companies' 30 April). [S1]
- One of three group borrowing entities tracked in the Loans Wiki; carried about £517,196 of facilities and £23,706 per month as at 21 Aug 2026. [S3]
- Operational trades historically covered: drylining, joinery, decorating, glazing partitions, floor sanding, painting, tiling, plumbing, electrics, fencing, roofing, gutters, mastic, drainage, LVT and carpet installation. [S5]
- Owner pay target referenced at £2k to £3k per month; £3,750 per month reported at the 18 Aug 2026 check-in. Directors' remuneration in the accounts was £46,772 for FY2025 (2024: £74,400). [S5][S1 p.15]

### Observations from the FY2025 accounts
- Revenue fell from £2.35m to £0.85m while the company capitalised £449,536 of leasehold improvements and £46,772 of software development. Combined with the £128,000 dividend, cash stayed thin (£20,938) and bank borrowing tripled. This is consistent with the Loans Wiki's picture of heavy short-term lending in 2024 to 2026.
- The leasehold improvements and the plant and machinery (which includes the woodworking machines under Haydock hire purchase) may relate to the furniture operation, which trades through a separate subsidiary. Which company owns the workshop lease and the machines is an open question shared with the Amfa Furniture article.
- **The woodworking machinery entered the books in FY2024, not FY2025.** The FY2024 accounts and CT600 show £294,729 of additions qualifying for the annual investment allowance, including the panel-shop machines bought from R&J Machinery on 9 November 2023 (Inventair MK1 and MK2, a Vitap K2, an Altendorf F45 panel saw and a Hebrock edgebander, about £181,595 together), a £29,000 Transit van, pneumatic tools and a fork truck, plus £67,774 of software development (WeAreWay Ltd). So the machinery behind OI-8 was capitalised in Fishbone Construction's books in the year to 29 April 2024. [S11 note 4][S13]

### Two findings, both from the adviser's own document rather than from the email

**1. Rev 1 contradicts itself on which decision was taken.**
"Points that bite" #2 and the options section record the 19 September decision as **keep 29 April**, no AA01.
"FY2026 open points" #1 says accounts to **30 April 2026** cannot be prepared without an AA01 extending the current
period — which presupposes the opposite decision. One of the two has to give, and it decides whether there is any
FY2026 action at all.

**2. The CT associated-company limits look wrong — the substantive finding.**
Rev 1: *"the marginal-relief limits divide by 6: the lower limit falls to about £41,700 (£250,000 / 6) and the
upper to £250,000 (£1.5m / 6)"*, repeated in "FY2026 open points" #3.

The division by six is right; the starting thresholds are not. Since the small profits rate was reintroduced on
**1 April 2023** the limits are **£50,000 lower** and **£250,000 upper**, each divided by the number of associated
companies. **£1.5m is the quarterly instalment payments threshold** (and the pre-2015 upper limit under the old
regime). Restated with six associates: **lower £8,333, upper £41,667** — not £41,700 and £250,000.

£1.5m ÷ 6 = £250,000 **is** correct as the instalments threshold, so the two regimes appear to have been set out
together. **Put to Alexey as a question, not asserted** — he is the specialist. For Construction the conclusion
may survive (above £41,667 it is the 25% main rate either way); for the other entities the difference is real,
since anything between £8,333 and £41,667 sits in marginal relief where Rev 1's figures place it in the small
profits band.

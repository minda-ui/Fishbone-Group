**WITNESS STATEMENT OF MINDAUGAS GAUDIESIUS**
Managing Director of the Defendant

1. I am Mindaugas Gaudiesius, Managing Director of Fishbone Construction Ltd, the Defendant and Claimant on the counterclaim ("Fishbone"). I make this statement from my own knowledge. Where I was told something by someone else, I say who told me.

**Background**

2. Fishbone is a labour supply company. We have worked with Macdonald Joinery & Construction Ltd ("Macdonald Joinery") for about 13 years. I deal mostly with its owner, David Macdonald, and mostly by telephone.

3. On the BBC Cardiff toilet refurbishment project at BBC Central Square (our reference FC2520), Macdonald Joinery was the main contractor and Fishbone was its subcontractor. Andrew Macdonald of Macdonald Joinery asked me by telephone that Fishbone take over the site management of the project. I do not remember the date of the call, but it was before his email of 24 November 2025. In that email he wrote that works would start on Monday 1 December 2025 and that its "Site Managers" for the pre-start meeting on 26 November would be Mr Davidans and me (exhibit MG11). I agreed to be named as a site manager. I attended the pre-start meeting on 26 November 2025.

4. I had known Mr Mikheil Davidans for a long time. He had worked for Fishbone on other sites before, and invoiced Fishbone through his company, MSEM UK Ltd. I offered him the site manager role on this project personally. The agreement was verbal, at £300 a day. There was no written agreement, and no fixed end date for his own engagement was agreed.

5. When I received his first invoice for this project, he had included fuel and accommodation. I spoke to him and agreed to pay them. I knew that he left the site around noon every Friday to travel home. In the same conversation I agreed to pay a full day for Fridays on one condition: that all the works were completed on schedule. That condition applied only to the Friday full days. It was not put in writing.

6. The draft programme dated 19 November 2025 produced for Macdonald Joinery, which Mr Davidans had, shows a snagging meeting on Monday 9 February 2026 and handover on Friday 13 February 2026 (exhibit MG2). On 4 February 2026 Dave Newell of J4 Projects, the project manager on the programme, asked Macdonald Joinery and the client by email whether "Thursday next week" suited for a "final site meeting for the WCs". On 6 February David Macdonald forwarded that email to me with the words "Please see date for snag and handover of the toilets", and I replied the same day: "Yes, Thursday perfect" (exhibit MG12). Thursday was 12 February 2026. There was no telephone call from J4 about this: the date was arranged by email only. The meeting on 12 February replaced the snagging meeting planned for 9 February. Works started on Monday 1 December 2025, and Mr Davidans was on site that morning carrying out inductions (exhibit MG11).

7. Fishbone supplied the labour. A team of two joiners, I. Bulakevych and R. Drulit, were provided to Fishbone through Torin Construction Ltd, and Raitis Bikovs worked as a Fishbone subcontractor. He worked on the Central Square project first: the site register, where he signs as "Raitis", shows him there on 2, 3, 4 and 5 December 2025. He started on the Roath Lock project on 8 December 2025, and the Torin joiners arrived at Central Square the same day (the register shows I. Bulakevych and R. Drulit from 8 December). His own emails put him back at Central Square on Wednesday 11 February and Monday 16 February 2026. As far as I am aware, those are the only days he worked at Central Square after he moved to Roath Lock. Fishbone moved him because Roath Lock needed one joiner and Central Square needed one more joiner, and Torin could provide a team of two joiners. So Mr Bikovs went to Roath Lock and the Torin joiners started at Central Square. Fishbone chose and paid for all of them. Mr Davidans, as site manager, directed them on site (exhibit MG4).

8. On his first day on site, I agreed with him that every day he would send me photographs of the progress and photographs of the site sign-in register, so that I could check who was on site. He did this through a Telegram group chat for the project, which I created on 1 December 2025. This is how Fishbone runs its projects: I set up a Telegram group for each one. The register photographs he sent run from 2 December 2025 to 6 February 2026 (exhibit MG1). Each photograph shows the sheet as it stood, so together they cover almost every working day: Mr Davidans appears on the register on 37 of the 39 working days from 2 December to 6 February, except 16 December and 19 January, which are not on any sheet he photographed. The group's members were Mr Davidans, Andrejus Prutkovas and me, and from 2 December Mr Bikovs. Anastasia, my business assistant, who prepared site progress reports for me, was also in the group until 7 January 2026. The chat shows that Mr Davidans posted photographs and videos on about 21 days between 2 December and 6 February, and that he never wrote any message in the group about defects, gaps, panels or labour. The group had a "Delivery" topic, which I created on 1 December 2025. This is how Fishbone keeps proof of deliveries: the site manager posts a photograph of each delivery, so that we can show when and what arrived if anyone asks. This is Fishbone's common practice: the same topics are used on other projects, for example FC2512 Parfums de Marly and FC2602 BBC Roath Lock Toilet upgrade, where the site managers post their deliveries (exhibit MG17). Mr Davidans knew the topics. I told him in person about such records when he first came to work with us, on the BBC W1 project. He was part of the management team that helped us control the site environment, and he valued records of deliveries, site progress pictures and the like, because they helped him do his job without having to chase people for answers. He knew how Fishbone operates. He was the site manager on FC2516 WoS Tissot Stratford in October and November 2025, in a group with the same four topics including Delivery, and he used the Sign-in and Progress pictures topics there every day. Deliveries did arrive at Tissot Stratford, but none were recorded in the Delivery topic by anyone. I do not know why. Recording them was his duty as site manager. On this job he posted nothing in the Delivery topic at any time (exhibit MG15), although Macdonald Joinery made deliveries to the site during the job. His last post was the sign-in sheet for 6 February, at 15:25. He posted nothing between 7 and 12 February (exhibit MG15). Nobody wrote in the group after 12 February 2026. I removed Mr Davidans from the group on 24 October 2026, to keep the evidence, and exported the chat; I exported it again in full on 2 November 2026 (exhibit MG15).

9. In December 2025 I was the site manager at the Roath Lock project and Mr Davidans was the site manager at Central Square. In the first working week of January 2026, Macdonald Joinery told me by telephone that they were unhappy with the costs and that one site manager was enough to cover five operatives across the two sites. I therefore left Mr Davidans as site manager on both sites, and I told him so in person. I do not remember the day. On 7 January 2026 Liam Price of EMCOR, the client's project manager, wrote to Macdonald Joinery, copying me, that he had been told Mr Davidans was managing both sites and that he needed a site manager at both projects at all times. On 12 January 2026 Andrew Macdonald confirmed that Oleksii Starovoitov was on site at Roath Lock as an English-speaking working foreman (exhibit MG13). Mr Starovoitov took over the foreman role at Roath Lock from that date. On 21 January 2026 Liam Price of EMCOR complained to Macdonald Joinery that the Roath Lock facilities management ("FM") team was not being briefed on progress. Andrew Macdonald asked me whether Mr Starovoitov was on site and whether he and Mr Davidans could brief the FM team each day. I replied to David Macdonald at 15:12: "We already spoke with Liam around 11.00 am today. Mikheil and Alex every morning will have little meetings with FM." (exhibit MG19). Mr Starovoitov did the morning briefings with the FM team. Mr Davidans spent most of his time at Central Square, and went to check progress at Roath Lock only when I asked him.

10. On or about 30 January 2026 there was a pre-handover meeting at the site. I did not attend it. Attending site meetings was part of Mr Davidans's duties as site manager. The same day, after the meeting, Mr Davidans telephoned me. He said he needed to finish the works and that he would be ready for handover. He did not say which works. He did not tell me about any defects or snagging items, and he did not ask for more labour or help.

**February 2026**

11. On 2 February 2026 I drove to Cardiff in Fishbone's van NJ17CYK with Andrejus Prutkovas. I arrived in Cardiff at about 15:00. We had a hotel not far from Roath Lock. I walked around the Roath Lock project. That day I went only to Roath Lock, not to Central Square. I did not see the operatives there, because their shifts had finished by that time, and I did not see Mr Davidans that day. On the morning of 3 February Andrejus Prutkovas and I met Dainius Drulia at Roath Lock, where he started that day. We spent three to four hours examining exactly what needed to be done there. I introduced him to the FM team and checked all the drawings. At 09:24 I called Mr Davidans to book parking at the BBC building. At 10:47 Mr Davidans's phone called mine. He told me that the parking was booked and that I could come over from Roath Lock. We then went to Central Square and visited Mr Davidans. I do not remember what time we arrived. We stayed about two to two and a half hours. We had a small talk and then walked around the building site. The works were progressing and no problems were raised during the walk-round. I asked him, as a routine question, whether he could finish. I do not remember exactly what he answered. Labour was not mentioned. Everything was fine at that time. It was a routine visit. At 13:55 I was at Central Square, which is why I called him again then. We left Cardiff when the visit to Central Square finished; I do not remember the time. We returned to Newcastle that night.

12. The photographs Mr Davidans posted in the Telegram group on 6 February 2026 showed the cubicles with the partitions up and the toilets and basins in. The dark panels behind the toilets are part of the toilet system and were in place. The white panels, which are Venesta laminated panels, and the five cubicle doors, which Macdonald Joinery made and delivered, had not yet been fitted. The photographs did not show the defects found later. I looked at them at the time and I thought only a small job was left. The doors and the white Venesta panels are made off site and delivered to site by Macdonald Joinery, and the hinges and handles are already fitted, so what remained was putting the white panels in place, which includes fitting the aluminium split battens they hang on, and hanging five doors. In my experience that takes two operatives three days at most. I do not know when the panels and doors arrived, because I was not there. Mr Davidans was the site manager and the site contact for deliveries, so deliveries were his to accept. I asked Mr Davidans whether he could finish: first when Andrejus Prutkovas and I visited him at Central Square on 3 February, then on my telephone call on 5 February, and then on each call before 12 February. On every call I asked him whether everything was all right, whether he needed anything, and whether he was on time to finish. I do not remember exactly what he answered on each call. The panels were made to exact sizes. If there are gaps between them once they are installed, the walls must have been built too long. That is my reasoning, from my routine visits to site; I did not see the walls being built. The bubbles on the doors (item 12 of the snagging list) are a manufacturing fault in the doors, which Macdonald Joinery made. They have nothing to do with this matter, and none of the costs in paragraph 27 relate to them.

Mr Drulia agreed. The total is £8,381.94 before VAT and CIS. All the bills have been paid from Fishbone's bank account: those of Snag Doctor, Mr Kogut, Mr Bikovs and Mr Starovoitov, and Mr Drulia's bills for the weeks ending 22 February and 1 March (paid on 6 and 13 March 2026).
